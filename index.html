Learn Excel & Sheets with Phoebe

A spreadsheet will hold anything and warn you about almost nothing. A lookup that matches the wrong customer, an ID stored as text, a pivot that counts payments as orders: each one prints a tidy number. Six sessions for analysts on spreadsheets as data work, taught by their silent failure modes rather than their buttons, with every formula checked against Microsoft's and Google's own help pages and every number computed from real order rows.

The six sessions 🧮 · from what one row is to an answer you can audit

The running case is Ferncastle, a constructed online kitchenware shop whose order export is real: derived, masked rows from the Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset by Olist. Imogen, the operations lead, wants three numbers for Monday: revenue by customer state, orders per customer and the late-delivery share. Dev, the analyst, builds them in a spreadsheet, and meets every trap the export really carries. Every formula works in both Excel and Google Sheets unless the page says which.

🧾 Session 1 · foundations

A table is not a grid

Grain, keys and one type per column. Ferncastle's real export opened and counted: 1,565 rows, 1,508 orders, and the three quiet paths to a plausible wrong number.

▶ Start here 🔗 Session 2 · core

Lookups and the join you didn't know you made

VLOOKUP's fourth argument, which is approximate when you leave it out, in both apps. XLOOKUP, INDEX and MATCH, and the first-match rule every lookup quietly applies.

exact, written out 🔤 Session 3 · core

Types that lie

Numbers stored as text, trailing spaces, and dates that depend on the computer's date setting. TRIM, VALUE and DATE, and the genes that were renamed because a spreadsheet guessed.

value and type 🧮 Session 4 · deeper

Pivots and what they hide

Group by, then count, sum or distinct count. Why counting rows is not counting orders, what Excel needs before it will count distinct, and totals that must reconcile.

count what you mean 🚪 Session 5 · deeper

When to leave

Row and cell limits read at source, and the better reasons to move: work that repeats, needs review or joins many tables. The hand-over to SQL and the command line.

know the exit 🔎 Session 6 · bench night

The formula-audit bench

A formula engine in the page runs Imogen's three questions five ways over the real rows and counts wrong rows, missing rows and double counts. Plus an anti-lever and a break button.
